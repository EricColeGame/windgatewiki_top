Losing a private furnace or garage stings, but it does not have to end your session. **Windgate public use locations** are shared facilities that any player can walk up to and use without owning them, and they exist so that one bad night does not erase hours of progress. There are three **Windgate public use locations** worth memorizing, and this guide breaks down each one: what it does, how to reach it, and how to use it without losing your materials to another player.

If you have ever stood over a wrecked base wondering where to smelt your ore or how to get your boat back, this walkthrough is for you. Public facilities are the safety net built into the map — you just have to know where they are before you need them.

## Windgate Public Use Locations at a Glance

Public use locations are shared structures that skip the ownership check entirely. A private furnace, garage, or dock only works for the player who built it. A public one works for everybody, which means two things: you always have a fallback when your setup is gone, and you should expect company while you use it.

According to community reports collected on the [Windgate shared facilities wiki walkthrough](https://www.windgatewiki.wiki/locations/Windgate-public-use-locations), the map contains three of these shared spots, and each one covers a different part of your progression loop.

| Location | Facility Type | Key Feature | Best Used When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stone Stuff Store | Shared furnace | Free smelting for any player | Your furnace is destroyed or you have not built one |
| Boat Store | Public garage + build ramp | Retrieve lost boats, build from a blueprint | Your private garage is gone and you need water access |
| Desert Town | Tunnel entrance | Waterfront access point beside the balcony | You need a quick route through the waterfront side of town |

The pattern is simple: one facility handles refining, one handles water travel, and one handles movement. Together they form a fallback chain that keeps you productive even after a total loss. Players who memorize all three recover noticeably faster than players who rebuild everything from scratch — mostly because they skip the resource cost of replacing infrastructure they never actually needed to own.

It is also worth saying plainly: because these spots are shared, they are crowded. Peak hours mean queues at the furnace and traffic at the boat ramp. Plan around that rather than being surprised by it.

## Stone Store Furnace: Free Smelting Access for Any Player

The stone stuff store is the most forgiving of the three spots. It hosts a fully functional furnace that any player can open and use, no ownership required and no permission check. If your own furnace blew up with the rest of your base, this is where your ore goes next.

The flow is identical to using a furnace you built yourself, which is exactly why it works as a drop-in replacement.

| Step | Action | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Travel to the stone stuff store | It is the landmark that anchors this shared furnace |
| 2 | Interact with the furnace | The interface opens normally — no ownership gate blocks you |
| 3 | Load your raw materials and fuel | Same smelting flow as a private furnace |
| 4 | Stay until the smelt finishes | Collect your output the moment it is ready |

**The one rule that matters most:** do not wander off mid-smelt. Public furnaces are shared, and materials left unattended are materials you may not get back. Community reports consistently flag this as the number one way players lose refined output at shared facilities — not to a bug, but to their own absence.

A practical habit: batch your smelting so you only make one trip. Load everything you need refined in a single session, stand there, and leave with finished materials instead of making three separate visits with three separate windows of risk. If the furnace is busy when you arrive, wait it out or come back outside peak hours rather than crowding someone else's smelt.

This location alone justifies learning the map. A free smelting point means ore you gathered is never dead weight in your inventory, even on a fresh start.

## Boat Store: Public Garage and Blueprint Build Ramp

The boat store packs two public features into a single area, both located down the ramp. This is the busiest of the Windgate public use locations because it solves the most painful loss in the game: a destroyed garage that takes your boat with it.

### The Public Garage

The garage at the boat store is a recovery point. Boats tied to destroyed private garages are retrieved here rather than vanishing permanently. That single detail changes how you should react to losing a base — instead of writing the boat off, you go get it.

### The Blueprint Build Ramp

Down the same ramp is a public build area where you can construct a boat using a blueprint. Community reports place this blueprint build spot in World 2.3, so if you are running that version, you can arrive empty-handed and leave with a working boat as long as you brought the blueprint with you.

| Your Situation | Where to Go | What to Do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Private garage destroyed, boat lost | Public garage at the boat store | Retrieve the boat |
| No boat at all, blueprint in inventory | Build area down the ramp | Construct a new boat |
| Both gone | Garage first, then the ramp | Retrieve what you can, then rebuild |

The order matters. Checking the garage first costs you nothing and sometimes hands you back the exact boat you thought you lost. Only after that should you spend a blueprint on a fresh build. Players who reverse the order end up building a replacement they did not need.

Carrying a blueprint at all times is the quiet advantage here. A blueprint in your inventory turns the boat store from a recovery point into a full-service dock.

## Desert Town Tunnel: The Waterfront Entrance Most Players Miss

The third public use location is the one people walk straight past. In Desert Town, a small tunnel entrance sits on the waterfront side of town, directly beside the balcony structure. There is no marker, no vendor, and no obvious reason to look — which is precisely why so many players never find it.

| Navigation Cue | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Region | Desert Town |
| Side of town | Waterfront side |
| Landmark | Beside the balcony |
| Entry method | Walk in and continue downward |

The reliable way to find it is to orient yourself toward the water and look for the balcony on that edge of town. If you can see both the water and the balcony, you are standing in the right area. From there, the entrance is a walk-in — no interaction prompt, no door to open. You simply head in and keep going down.

Because it is easy to miss, treat it as a landmark to learn rather than a destination to stumble onto. Once you have walked it a couple of times, the location sticks, and you will start using it as a natural route through the waterfront side of Desert Town instead of going around.

## How to Use Windgate Public Use Locations Wisely

Shared facilities come with shared risk. The good news is that nearly every risk has a simple counter, and most of them come down to timing and attention rather than skill.

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Output taken while you are away | Stay near the furnace for the entire smelt |
| Crowded facility slowing you down | Visit outside peak hours |
| Layout changed after a world update | Re-confirm each spot after a major update |
| Panic-rebuilding a boat you could have retrieved | Check the public garage before crafting anything |
| Arriving at the build ramp without materials | Keep a blueprint and boat materials on hand |

A routine that works:

- Memorize all three locations before you actually need them, ideally on a quiet session.
- After losing a private garage, check the boat store's public garage first.
- Park yourself at the stone store furnace until the smelt completes.
- Keep a blueprint in your inventory so the boat store ramp is always an option.
- Re-verify public spots after each major world update, since shared facilities can shift between versions.

**Player experience note:** the version-specific detail about the blueprint build ramp is community-sourced, not something the game announces in a menu. Treat it as a strong starting point and confirm it in your own world. Public layouts are not guaranteed to stay identical forever, and a spot that works perfectly today may be relocated after a large update.

The bigger mindset shift is treating these locations as infrastructure rather than emergency stops. A player who knows where to smelt, where to recover a boat, and how to move through Desert Town is never truly starting over. Losses cost time, not progress — and that is a much easier hole to climb out of.

## FAQ

**Q: What are the main Windgate public use locations?**

There are three: the shared furnace at the stone stuff store, the boat store's public garage and blueprint build ramp, and the small tunnel in Desert Town beside the waterfront balcony. All three are open to every player with no ownership requirement.

**Q: Where do I get my boat back if my private garage is destroyed?**

Head to the public garage at the boat store, down the ramp. Boats lost when a private garage is destroyed are retrieved there rather than being gone for good. Check the garage before you spend resources on a new build.

**Q: Can I build a boat at a public location?**

Yes. Community reports confirm a public build spot down the boat store's ramp where you can construct a boat using a blueprint, noted in World 2.3. Bring the blueprint and the required materials with you, since the ramp is a construction area, not a shop.

**Q: Is the stone store furnace really open to everyone?**

Yes — the furnace at the stone stuff store can be used by any player, with no ownership check. Just stay at the location until your smelting finishes so you can collect your output immediately rather than leaving it unattended at a shared facility.
