## Rock Climbing: Half Day vs. Full Day

The half-day climbing tour is the classic entry point. It runs about four hours at a single-pitch destination close to town, and the curriculum covers balance, trusting your feet, and belay techniques — the three skills that quietly determine whether you enjoy climbing or fight it.

The full-day version spends roughly seven hours at the crag and layers in face climbing alongside traditional crack climbing. It suits someone who has already sampled the sport and wants mileage, technique work, and rope management in one block. A private booking lets you steer that curriculum entirely.

| Feature | Half day | Full day |
|---|---|---|
| Time on route | ~4 hours | ~7 hours at the crag |
| Focus | Balance, footwork, belay basics | Technique, rope work, face and crack climbing |
| Style | Single-pitch destinations near Moab | Mixed face and traditional crack terrain |
| Customizable | Group rates available | Private tours fully customizable |
| Skill level | Beginner, intermediate, advanced | Beginner, intermediate, advanced |

Anyone who wants to move beyond top-roping can step into the advanced courses, which cover lead climbing, anchor rigging, cleaning, lead belaying with ATC and Gri-Gri devices, multi-pitch technique, and desert tower preparation.

## Canyoneering and Rappelling: The Gentlest Entry Point

If the idea of climbing a vertical wall makes your palms sweat, canyoneering is the softer on-ramp. It blends hiking, scrambling, boulder-hopping, and rappelling to descend canyons carved by ancient rivers. The half-day options are explicitly rated for beginners and described as perfect for families and first-time rappellers.

Rappelling tours work the same way. Guests are backed up on a safety line controlled by the guide during every technical section, which means a first-timer can lean back over an arch with a redundant system holding them. That single detail is why so many people book a rappel trip and a canyon trip in the same weekend.

## Desert Towers: How Beginners Move Up the Ladder

Desert towers are the postcard image of Moab, and the good news is that the ladder starts low. Introductory multi-pitch routes such as Looking Glass Rock let new climbers practice rope management, lead belaying, gear cleaning, and a 140-foot rappel through an arch — all before they ever stand on a true tower summit.

From there, the progression is well documented by the operator's own route list, and it is worth treating as a multi-season plan rather than a single vacation.

| Route | Grade | Pitches | Skill level | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Looking Glass Rock (East Rib) | 5.6 | 3 | Beginner to advanced | The ideal first multi-pitch; low-angle slab |
| Space Tower (Hallow Souls) | 5.9+ | 2 | Intermediate+ | Chimney climbing in wild positions |
| Ancient Art (Stolen Chimney) | 5.8 A0 or 5.10+ | 4 | Intermediate+ | Corkscrew summit at the Fisher Towers |
| Castleton Tower (Kor-Ingalls / North Chimney) | 5.9 | 4 or 3 | Intermediate+ | The iconic 50 Classic Climbs monolith |
| Lighthouse Tower (Lonely Vigil) | 5.10b | 4 | Intermediate+ | Recommended next step after Castleton |
| Bounce Test | 5.9 A0 or 5.10 | 7+ | Intermediate+ | Roughly 1,300 feet of roped climbing; walk-off descent |

## Photography Tours: Adventure Without the Adrenaline

Not every beginner wants exposure. Windgate also runs sunrise and sunset photography tours into Arches and Canyonlands, aimed at people who have been shooting in auto mode and want to understand aperture, ISO, shutter speed, composition, and timing.

The differentiator here is the guide himself. Owner and lead guide Eric Odenthal has worked as a professional photographer for more than a decade, so guests get a guide and a photography instructor in one person. Sunrise trips are four hours and require an early alarm; sunset trips cover more ground with short hikes and no pre-dawn wake-up.

| Time block | What happens |

## Practical Tips Before You Book

- **Book the half day first.** Four hours is long enough to know whether you love it and short enough to leave room in your itinerary.
- **Rent the shoes.** At $7 per pair per day, rental climbing shoes beat packing your own for a single session.
- **Respect the age minimum.** Eight years is the floor across climbing, canyoneering, and rappelling tours, so plan childcare accordingly.
- **Ask about a private charter.** A 25% surcharge buys a customized itinerary and the guide's undivided attention, which is often worth it for families.
- **Bring water and layers.** Desert temperatures swing hard between morning and afternoon, and canyon bottoms stay cool.
- **Check the season.** Entrajo, for example, runs in summer only because the experience depends on pooled rainwater.
- **Read the full tour list before deciding.** The [Windgate Adventures official site](https://www.windgateadventures.com/complete-secondary-pages) breaks down every route, duration, and skill rating in one place.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need any climbing experience for a Windgate beginner guide trip?**
No. Half-day climbing, half-day canyoneering, rappelling, and photography tours are all rated for beginners, and guides provide instruction from the ground up. Advanced courses exist for guests who already climb.

**What is the minimum age?**
Eight years old on climbing, canyoneering, and rappelling tours. There is no stated minimum for the photography tours, which involve short hikes rather than technical terrain.

**What gear do I need to bring?**
Very little. Harness, helmet, and belay device are supplied on climbing trips, with gloves added on canyoneering and rappelling tours. Climbing shoes can be rented for $7 per pair per day.

**How much more does a private tour cost?**
A chartered private tour carries a 25% additional charge over the standard rate, and it lets you customize the curriculum or route selection to your group's goals.
