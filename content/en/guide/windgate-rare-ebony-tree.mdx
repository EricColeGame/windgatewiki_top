Windgate's forests are generous. Common softwoods are everywhere, and most players stop paying attention to tree species after their first hour of play. Ebony breaks that pattern completely.

They grow large, with dark bark and leaves that make them stand out once you know what you're looking for. That biome restriction is the whole story — it means you cannot stumble into ebony by accident near most starter regions.

Rarity changes how you play. When a resource is common, you harvest on impulse. When it's rare, you plan a route, pack the right tools, and decide in advance what you're willing to leave behind. ## Where Does the Windgate Rare Ebony Tree Spawn?

There are exactly two answers: Savannas and Tropics. Everything else in the game world is off the table. That's a short list, which is good news — it means you can narrow your search dramatically instead of wandering the entire map.

### Savannas: Open Ground and Long Sightlines

Savannas are the friendlier hunting ground for most players. The terrain is open, so you can scan from a ridge, a hilltop, or while riding across the plains and spot a large dark-barked tree from a long way off. Ebony tends to read as a tall, dark shape against lighter grass and scrub.

The tradeoff is exposure. Open biomes usually mean less cover, so you'll want to keep an eye on your surroundings while chopping.

### Tropics: Dense Cover and Harder Searching

Tropics hide their trees. Foliage is thick, sightlines are short, and you'll often be right next to an ebony trunk before you notice it. The upside is that Tropics tend to be more forgiving in terms of cover — useful if you're harvesting in a busy area.

The practical tip: in Tropics, look at bark, not canopy. Dark trunks stand out against the greener, lighter vegetation long before the leaves come into view.

| Biome | How Ebony Appears | Visibility | Main Challenge | Player Tip |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Savanna | Tall, isolated dark trunk | High — visible from ridges | Open ground, less cover | Sweep from high points first |
| Tropics | Dark trunk inside dense foliage | Low — often spotted late | Poor sightlines, harder navigation | Scan for bark, not leaves |
| Forest / Plains | Does not spawn | — | Wasted travel time | Skip these biomes entirely |

## How to Tell a Windgate Rare Ebony Tree Apart From Ordinary Trees

Misidentification is the most common reason players return from a trip empty-handed. They chop a large tree, get common wood, and assume ebony simply wasn't there. The fix is a short checklist you run every time you spot a big tree in a Savanna or Tropics.

| Feature | Ebony Tree | Typical Common Tree |
|---|---|---|
| Bark color | Dark, near-black | Lighter browns and grays |
| Trunk size | Large, thick | Varies, usually slimmer |
| Canopy | Distinctive leaf pattern | Familiar, uniform foliage |
| Biome | Savannas and Tropics only | Widespread |
| Overall read | Stands out as a dark landmark | Blends into the treeline |

A few habits that make identification faster:

- **Check the biome first.** If you're not in a Savanna or Tropics, stop looking. No amount of searching will produce ebony elsewhere.
- **Trust color over size.** Big trees are everywhere; near-black bark is the stronger signal.
- **Compare against neighbors.** Ebony reads darker than almost everything growing around it.
- **Note the landmark.** When you find one, record the location so you can return without re-searching the whole biome.

- **Combine trips.** If you're already traveling to a Savanna or Tropics for another resource, add ebony to the itinerary instead of making a separate journey.
- **Bring storage.** A rare resource you can't carry home is not a resource at all.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where does the Windgate rare ebony tree spawn?**
Only in Savannas and Tropics, according to the Windgate Wiki. If you're anywhere else in the world, you won't find one, no matter how long you search.

**How rare is ebony compared to other trees?**
It's classified as a rare tree type, and player experience suggests it doesn't appear in every eligible area. Expect to search a few separate locations before you find one.

**How do I identify an ebony tree quickly?**
Look for near-black bark and a large trunk in a Savanna or Tropics biome. Bark color is the fastest signal — leaves confirm it, but they're often the last thing you see.

**Should I harvest every ebony tree I find?**
Community reports suggest ebony regrows slowly, so many players leave a few trees standing to keep a location productive. Harvest what you need, mark the spot, and return later.

**Is ebony worth a dedicated trip?**
For most players, yes. Its scarcity makes it valuable for building, crafting, and trading — and planning a single efficient run beats several improvised ones.
