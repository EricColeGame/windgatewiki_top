## The Three Broad Bands of Windgate Wood Types

Almost every wood-gated system sorts trees into three loose bands: common filler woods, dependable mid-tier hardwoods, and rare specialty woods. Windgate wood types follow that same logic, and knowing which band you are dealing with tells you most of what you need to know before you swing an axe.

### Common softwoods

These are the trees you trip over on your way to somewhere else. They grow in bulk, respawn quickly, and are easy to process. Their per-log value is modest, but their reliability makes them the backbone of early progress and a safe fallback when a rare spawn refuses to show up.

### Mid-tier hardwoods

This is where the real grinding happens. Mid-tier hardwoods take longer to find and longer to cut, but they pay enough to justify a dedicated run. Most players who feel "stuck" are actually just farming softwoods out of habit instead of stepping up a band.

### Rare and event woods

Rare woods are the ones people talk about. They appear in specific conditions, often in limited windows or unusual locations, and they carry premium value. The catch is variance — you can spend a long session hunting one and come home with nothing. Smart players treat these as bonus targets, not as the plan.

| Band | Effort to find | Effort to haul | Best used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Common softwoods | Very low | Low | Warm-up runs, filling gaps, learning routes |
| Mid-tier hardwoods | Moderate | Moderate | Primary income and steady progression |
| Rare and event woods | High | High | Big paydays, trading, milestone goals |

## How to Identify Windgate Wood Types in the Field

Misidentification is the quiet killer of efficient runs. You spot a promising trunk, spend several minutes on it, and only realize at the mill that it was a common species with a similar silhouette.

- **Bark texture first.** Smooth, rough, ridged, or peeling bark separates most species faster than anything else.
- **Leaf shape and color.** Broadleaf, needle, and unusual color variants are strong signals of band.
- **Trunk width and height.** Premium trees tend to be visibly chunkier or taller than filler trees.
- **Glow and particle effects.** Anything luminous or animated is almost always a specialty wood.
- **Location context.** Biome and elevation are clues; the same species rarely appears everywhere.
- **Sound and animation cues.** Some trees creak, sway, or shimmer differently when you approach.

| Cue | Reliable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Bark texture | High | Best single indicator in most cases |
| Leaf shape and color | High | Fails at night or in fog |
| Trunk size | Medium | Young premium trees can look ordinary |
| Glow effects | Very high | Nearly always means specialty wood |
| Biome location | Medium | Helpful for narrowing, not confirming |

Community reports suggest that the fastest way to learn these cues is to spend one session deliberately identifying trees without cutting them. It feels wasteful. It pays off immediately afterward.

## Farming Tips That Actually Save Time

Efficiency at Windgate is less about chopping speed and more about route planning. Most lost time happens in travel, not in cutting.

| Goal | Approach | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Maximize profit per hour | Pick one band and run it repeatedly | Removes decision time between trips |
| Learn the map fast | Follow a fixed loop every session | Muscle memory beats improvisation |
| Avoid empty returns | Carry a filler wood as backup | Guarantees the trip is never a total loss |
| Reduce hauling waste | Match wood weight to your capacity | Prevents overflow that gets discarded |
| Chase rare spawns | Farm a nearby mid-tier while waiting | Turns dead time into income |

A few habits separate efficient players from busy ones:

- **Set a capacity target before you leave.** Know how many logs you can carry and stop when you hit it.
- **Bank early, bank often.** Long single hauls feel productive but risk more than they reward.
- **Keep a written tier list.** A short note of which species are worth cutting saves real time.
- **Upgrade tools before chasing premium wood.** Better equipment changes which tiers are viable.
- **Track your best runs.** Knowing your personal record per route shows which band deserves your hours.

## FAQ

**What are Windgate wood types, exactly?**
They are the categories of trees and logs that the Windgate mechanic recognizes and rewards. Community reports group them into common softwoods, mid-tier hardwoods, and rare or specialty woods, each with different value, weight, and farming difficulty.

**Which Windgate wood types should I farm first?**
Start with common softwoods to learn the map, then move into mid-tier hardwoods as soon as your tools and capacity allow. Mid-tier is where steady income lives. Rare woods are a bonus target, not a starting strategy.

**Do I need every wood type to pass Windgate?**
Based on player experience, no. What matters is bringing the right type for the situation rather than a random assortment. A focused load of one strong band usually beats a mixed inventory of everything.

**How often do Windgate wood types respawn?**
Respawn timing varies by species and band — commons return quickly, while rare and event woods can take considerably longer or require specific conditions. Rotating between two or three routes is the most reliable way to avoid waiting around.

Mastering Windgate wood types is ultimately a study in patience and planning. Learn the bands, read the trees, build a loop, and the gate stops being an obstacle and starts being a routine.
